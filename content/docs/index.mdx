xark compiles a Noir circuit into a ~256-byte Groth16 proof and a self-contained Solana verifier
that checks it through the native `alt_bn128` syscalls. A program acts on *"this statement is true"* —
an age, a balance, a Merkle membership — without ever seeing the private data behind it.

It lowers **ACIR, not Noir source**: `nargo` compiles your circuit to ACIR, and xark lowers the
supported opcodes into R1CS and proves them with Arkworks Groth16 over BN254.

## What makes it different

- **It refuses what it can't prove soundly.** Unsupported opcodes are rejected *eagerly, before any
  setup or proving cost*, with a specific error — never silently mis-proved. See
  [supported opcodes](/docs/reference/supported-opcodes).
- **A real trusted setup.** Beyond dev keys, xark runs a multi-contributor phase-2
  [MPC ceremony](/docs/trust/trusted-setup) with Schnorr proofs of contribution and δ-consistency
  checks.
- **Machine-checked where it counts.** The gadgets and the ACIR→R1CS lowering carry
  [Lean 4 proofs](/docs/trust/formal-proofs-and-audit); the on-chain verifier is checked with Kani;
  every proof is cross-verified against [snarkjs](/docs/reference/snarkjs).
- **A tiny, `no_std` on-chain verifier** with a [reproducible build](/docs/trust/reproducible-builds)
  you can hash-match against what's actually deployed.

<Callout type="warn" title="Status: experimental">
xark is **experimental and not externally audited**, and its on-chain verifier has never been
deployed to mainnet. The formal proofs and test suite are real, but they are not a substitute for an
external audit. Don't hold value on it yet.
</Callout>
