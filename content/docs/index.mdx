title: Introduction

xark compiles a circuit written as ordinary Rust into a ~256-byte Groth16 proof and a
self-contained Solana verifier that checks it through the native `alt_bn128` syscalls.
A program acts on *"this statement is true"* — an age, a balance, a Merkle membership —
without ever seeing the private data behind it.

There is no separate circuit DSL: you write a `#![no_std]` Rust function over `Field`
values, `xark` drives `rustc` to type-check it, extracts its **MIR**, lowers that to R1CS,
and proves it with Arkworks Groth16 over BN254. Gadgets — hashes, curves, signatures — are
ordinary Rust crates that lower to the same small constraint set.

## What makes it different

- **Gadgets are plain Rust.** No per-gadget backend opcodes, no separate circuit language.
  `xark-sha256`, `xark-poseidon`, `xark-secp256k1` are crates you add with `cargo`, inlined
  into your circuit at compile time. See [Gadgets](/docs/gadgets).
- **It refuses what it can't prove soundly.** Anything outside the accepted Rust subset is
  rejected *eagerly, before any setup or proving cost* — as a real `rustc` diagnostic with a
  source span — never silently mis-proved.
- **A real trusted setup.** Beyond dev keys, xark runs a multi-contributor phase-2
  [MPC ceremony](/docs/trust/trusted-setup) with Schnorr proofs of contribution and δ-consistency
  checks.
- **Machine-checked where it counts.** The gadgets carry [Lean 4 proofs](/docs/trust/formal-proofs-and-audit);
  the on-chain verifier is checked with Kani; every proof is cross-verified against
  [snarkjs](/docs/reference/snarkjs).
- **A tiny, `no_std` on-chain verifier** with a [reproducible build](/docs/trust/reproducible-builds)
  you can hash-match against what's actually deployed.

<Callout type="warn" title="Status: experimental">
xark is **experimental and not externally audited**, and its on-chain verifier has never been
deployed to mainnet. The formal proofs and test suite are real, but they are not a substitute for an
external audit. Don't hold value on it yet.
</Callout>
