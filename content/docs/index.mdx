title: "Introduction"
description: "Groth16 proving backend for Noir, verified on Solana."
---

xark is a Groth16 proving backend for Noir circuits. You write a circuit in Noir; xark lowers it to
R1CS, runs the Groth16 setup, produces proofs, and exports an on-chain verifier crate with the
verifying key baked in.

The point of a zero-knowledge proof is that a Solana program can act on a statement being *true*
without ever seeing the private data behind it. Prove you're over 18, that a balance covers a
withdrawal, that a value sits in a Merkle tree — the chain checks the proof, not the secret.

Verification happens natively: xark targets Groth16 over the BN254 curve, which Solana verifies
through its `alt_bn128` syscalls. Proofs are ~256 bytes and verify in a few hundred k compute units,
no matter how large the underlying computation was.
