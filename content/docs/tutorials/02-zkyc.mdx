---
title: "02 · zKYC"
description: Range proofs put to work. Prove you pass a KYC check (age, net worth, nationality, a valid document) without showing your ID, against a salted commitment and a policy the program picks.
---

In this tutorial you'll prove to a Solana program that you pass its KYC policy: 18 or older today,
worth at least 500,000, a national of a country it allows, with an ID document that hasn't expired.
The program learns that, and nothing else: not your birth date, your net worth, your nationality or
your document number.

[01 · Over 9000](/docs/tutorials/01-over-9000) taught the [range proof](/docs/learn-zk/range-proofs)
on a number nobody cared about. Here range proofs do real work: an age is a comparison of dates, and
a minimum net worth a comparison of amounts. Around them you'll add three more privacy tools:

- a **set membership check**, so the program learns that your country is on its list, not which
  one it is;
- a **salted commitment**, so the identity is fixed by an issuer and the only trace of it onchain
  reveals nothing;
- the **holder's wallet** as a public input, so no one else can submit your proof.

Along the way you'll meet **typed inputs** (dates, countries, a set of countries) that xark
**packs** into few field elements, and a **policy** as a public input, which the program builds
itself so the verifier picks the rules.

By the end you'll have the same pieces as in 01: a circuit and its tests, a TypeScript client, a
Solana program, and an end-to-end test that sends a proof to the program and sees it accepted for
the right wallet, under the program's policy, on the right day, and refused otherwise.

<Callout title="Prerequisites">
Everything from [01's prerequisites](/docs/tutorials/01-over-9000): stable Rust 1.89 or newer,
`xark-cli` 0.3, Node 22 or newer, and the Anza CLI for `cargo-build-sbf`. Nothing new. You don't
need 01's files, but this tutorial assumes you've done it and doesn't re-explain its commands.
</Callout>

<Step>
### Describe the identity

An issuer (a KYC provider, a government portal) checks your documents once and gives you what it
found as an `Identity`. Replace `zkyc/src/lib.rs` with:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
use xark::prelude::*;

/// A calendar day from 1900 through 2100.
pub type Day = Days<1900, 2100>;

/// What an issuer attests about a holder.
#[input]
pub struct Identity {
    /// The ID document's number.
    pub document: Text,
    pub born: Day,
    pub nationality: Choice<Country>,
    pub net_worth: u64,
    /// The ID document's expiry.
    pub expires: Day,
    /// The issuer's random secret: it hides the commitment.
    pub salt: Field,
}

#[input]
impl Identity {
    /// The issuer's commitment: all a verifier sees of the holder.
    pub fn commitment(&self) -> Field {
        xark_poseidon2::hash(self.to_fields())
    }
}
```

The commitment is the Poseidon2 hash of all six fields, and it's the only part of the identity that
ever goes onchain. You can prove things about the identity behind it as often as you like,
to any verifier.

<Callout title="Why the commitment is salted">
A hash hides its input only if the input can't be guessed. An identity mostly can: there are about
73,000 days in two centuries, 249 countries and a handful of round net worths. Without the salt,
anyone who saw the commitment could hash every plausible birth date, country and net worth until
one matched, and read your identity off it. The `salt` is a random field element the issuer picks
and gives only to you. With it in the hash, there's nothing to enumerate: the commitment reveals
nothing about the identity. See [Commitments](/docs/gadgets/commitments).
</Callout>

Each field has a type that says what it is, and xark checks it on the way in:

- `Text`: up to 31 bytes of UTF-8, such as `"A1938274"`.
- `Choice<Country>`: one ISO 3166 country; in TypeScript, `Country.US` or `"US"`.
- `u64`: range-checked to 64 bits, as in 01.
- `Day`, a `Days<1900, 2100>`: in the circuit, a count of days since 1900-01-01, so dates compare
  as numbers; in tests it's a string like `"1990-01-01"`, and in TypeScript a `Date`.

See [Everyday types](/docs/reference/inputs#everyday-types) for the rest.
</Step>

<Step>
### Describe the policy

Now the verifier's side: what it requires, on which day. Append to `zkyc/src/lib.rs`:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
/// What a verifier requires, checked on `today`.
#[input]
pub struct Policy {
    pub today: Day,
    /// The latest birth date allowed.
    pub born_by: Day,
    pub min_net_worth: u64,
    pub allowed: Bitfield<Country>,
}

#[input]
impl Policy {
    /// On `today`: at least `min_age` years old, worth at least `min_net_worth`, a
    /// national of one of `allowed`, with a document that hasn't expired.
    #[cfg(not(xark))]
    pub fn on(
        today: impl Into<Day>,
        min_age: u16,
        min_net_worth: u64,
        allowed: impl Into<Bitfield<Country>>,
    ) -> Policy {
        let today = today.into();
        Policy {
            today,
            born_by: today.years_before(min_age),
            min_net_worth,
            allowed: allowed.into(),
        }
    }
}
```

`Bitfield<Country>` is a set of countries, one bit each. `#[input]` on an `impl` exports its
methods to the TypeScript client, so the issuer computes the commitment, and the holder the policy,
with this same Rust. `#[cfg(not(xark))]` leaves `Policy::on` out of the circuit build: the circuit
never calls it; it only compares.

A Groth16 public input is a field element, and each one costs the program work to check. So
`#[input]` **packs** a struct's narrow fields: consecutive integers, days and choices share one
field element, the first in the lowest bits, while they fit in 253 bits. `Policy` is two field
elements, not four: `today`, `born_by` and `min_net_worth` in one (17 + 17 + 64 bits), and the
249-bit `allowed` in its own. `Identity` is three, so its commitment hashes three. Try it:

</Step>

<Step>
### Write the circuit and its tests

Append the circuit and three tests:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
#[circuit]
pub fn zkyc(id: Private<Identity>, commitment: Public<Field>, policy: Public<Policy>) {
    require_eq(id.commitment(), commitment);
    require(id.born <= policy.born_by);
    require(id.net_worth >= policy.min_net_worth);
    require(id.expires >= policy.today);
    id.nationality.require_in(policy.allowed);
}

#[cfg(test)]
mod tests {
    use super::*;
    use Country::*;

    fn holder(nationality: Country, born: &str, net_worth: u64, expires: &str) -> Identity {
        Identity {
            document: "A1938274".into(),
            born: born.into(),
            nationality: nationality.into(),
            net_worth,
            expires: expires.into(),
            salt: Field::from_le_bytes(&[0x2a; 31]),
        }
    }

    fn policy() -> Policy {
        Policy::on("2026-10-22", 18, 500_000, [DE, FR, GB, US])
    }

    fn check(id: Identity) -> Result<(), xark::prover::ProveError> {
        zkyc(id, id.commitment(), policy())
    }

    #[test]
    fn a_compliant_holder_passes() {
        check(holder(US, "1990-01-01", 1_000_000, "2030-01-01")).unwrap();
        // Every limit is inclusive: 18 today, exactly 500,000, the document's last day.
        check(holder(GB, "2008-10-22", 500_000, "2026-10-22")).unwrap();
    }

    #[test]
    fn each_check_can_fail() {
        assert!(check(holder(US, "2008-10-23", 1_000_000, "2030-01-01")).is_err()); // 18 tomorrow
        assert!(check(holder(US, "1990-01-01", 499_999, "2030-01-01")).is_err());
        assert!(check(holder(US, "1990-01-01", 1_000_000, "2026-10-21")).is_err()); // expired
        assert!(check(holder(JP, "1990-01-01", 1_000_000, "2030-01-01")).is_err());
    }

    #[test]
    fn another_identity_fails() {
        let id = holder(US, "1990-01-01", 1_000_000, "2030-01-01");
        let other = holder(US, "1990-01-02", 1_000_000, "2030-01-01").commitment();
        assert!(zkyc(id, other, policy()).is_err());
    }
}
```

The first `require_eq` is the one that matters. In 01 the prover could pick any number. Here every
value the other checks read has to be the one inside the commitment, so the prover can't choose
convenient ones. Each of the other lines proves one rule while hiding the value it checks:

- **Age is a range proof on dates.** A `Day` is a count of days since 1900-01-01, range-checked to
  17 bits as it's read, so `id.born <= policy.born_by` is 01's comparison on two 17-bit numbers.
  The verifier learns "born on or before `born_by`", never the birth date. The expiry check,
  `id.expires >= policy.today`, works the same way.
- **Minimum net worth is a 64-bit range proof.** `id.net_worth >= policy.min_net_worth` compares
  two range-checked `u64`s. It proves the holder has at least the minimum, never how much.
- **The nationality is checked against an allow-list without naming it.** The nationality is a
  private `Choice<Country>`, the country's position in the list; `allowed` is a public
  `Bitfield<Country>`, one bit per country. `require_in` splits `allowed` into its 249 bits and
  multiplies one factor per country, each zero only where the nationality is that country and that
  country's bit is set; the product must be zero. The verifier sees only the allowed set, not
  which member of it you are.

None of this is a new kind of constraint: the comparisons are [range
proofs](/docs/learn-zk/range-proofs), and the allow-list check rests on bit decompositions too.

The tests call the circuit's own Rust (`id.commitment()`, `Policy::on`) to build their inputs, as
the client will.
</Step>

<Step>
### Bind the proof to the holder's wallet

So far a proof says that *someone* holding this credential passes. Anyone who saw it onchain could
submit it as their own. To stop that, make the holder's wallet a public input. A Solana address is
32 bytes, which the circuit takes as `xark_hash::Hash`.

Add the import at the top of `src/lib.rs`:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
use xark_hash::Hash;
```

and give the circuit a fourth input:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
#[circuit]
pub fn zkyc(
    id: Private<Identity>,
    commitment: Public<Field>,
    policy: Public<Policy>,
    holder: Public<Hash>,
) {
```

In the tests, add a wallet after `use Country::*;`:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
    const HOLDER: [u8; 32] = [7; 32];
```

and pass `HOLDER` as the last argument to both calls of `zkyc`:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
        zkyc(id, id.commitment(), policy(), HOLDER)
```

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
        assert!(zkyc(id, other, policy(), HOLDER).is_err());
```

Run the tests again:

```console
$ xark test zkyc
xark building circuit `zkyc`
warning: unused variable: `holder`
...
error: public input `holder.hi` is declared but no constraint uses it
  = note: if only the verifier supplies it (a recipient, a nonce), say so with `bind(it)`: the proof then verifies only with the value it was made for. Otherwise use it in a constraint, or remove it from the signature
```

xark refuses the circuit. Groth16 only checks a public input through the constraints that use
it, so an input no constraint touches would verify with any value. Nothing needs to compute with
`holder`; it only has to be tied to the proof. That's what `bind` does. Add it as the circuit's
last line:

```rust title="zkyc/src/lib.rs"
    id.nationality.require_in(policy.allowed);
    bind(holder);
}
```

```ts title="app/zkyc.test.ts"
import { readFileSync } from "node:fs";
import { beforeAll, expect, test } from "vitest";
import { CheckFailed, Country, Field, Identity, Policy, Zkyc, type ZkycProver } from "./zkyc";

// What `xark build` and `xark setup` wrote. A web app would serve this
// directory and pass its URL instead.
const out = "../zkyc/target/xark/zkyc/";
const read = (file: string) => new Uint8Array(readFileSync(out + file));

let prover: ZkycProver;
beforeAll(async () => {
  prover = await Zkyc.prover(
    {
      circuit: read("circuit.xbc"),
      provingKey: read("pk.bin"),
      sources: readFileSync(out + "sources.json", "utf8"),
      client: read("client.wasm"), // the crate's Rust: Identity.commitment, Policy.on
    },
    { digest: Zkyc.digest },
  );
});

/** The issuer's credential for a holder, with a random salt. */
const credential = (changes: Partial<Identity> = {}): Identity => ({
  document: "A1938274",
  born: new Date("1990-01-01"),
  nationality: Country.US,
  netWorth: 1_000_000,
  expires: new Date("2030-01-01"),
  salt: new Field(crypto.getRandomValues(new Uint8Array(31))),
  ...changes,
});

/** The program's policy on `today`. */
const programs = (today: string) => Policy.on(new Date(today), 18, 500_000, ["DE", "FR", "GB", "US"]);

test("a compliant holder has a proof", async () => {
  const id = credential();
  const commitment = Identity.commitment(id);
  const policy = programs("2026-10-22");
  expect(policy.bornBy).toEqual(new Date("2008-10-22"));

  const holder = new Uint8Array(32).fill(7);
  const proof = prover.prove({ id, commitment, policy, holder });

  // The verifier sees the public inputs, and nothing of `id`.
  const verifier = await Zkyc.verifier(read("vk.bin"));
  expect(verifier.verify(proof.toBytes(), { commitment, policy, holder })).toBe(true);
});

test("an identity that fails the policy has no proof", () => {
  const policy = programs("2026-10-22");
  const prove = (changes: Partial<Identity>) => () => {
    const id = credential(changes);
    prover.prove({ id, commitment: Identity.commitment(id), policy, holder: new Uint8Array(32) });
  };
  expect(prove({ born: new Date("2008-10-23") })).toThrow(CheckFailed); // 18 tomorrow
  expect(prove({ netWorth: 499_999 })).toThrow(/id\.net_worth >= policy\.min_net_worth/);
  expect(prove({ nationality: Country.JP })).toThrow(/id\.nationality\.require_in/);
});
```

Then replace `program/src/lib.rs` with the entrypoint:

```rust title="program/src/lib.rs"
#![cfg_attr(not(test), no_std)]

use pinocchio::{
    AccountView, Address, ProgramResult,
    error::ProgramError,
    program_entrypoint,
    sysvars::{Sysvar, clock::Clock},
};
use zkyc_verifier::{Proof, verify};

program_entrypoint!(process_instruction);
#[cfg(not(test))]
pinocchio::nostd_panic_handler!();
#[cfg(not(test))]
pinocchio::no_allocator!();

/// Accounts: the holder, a signer. Data: proof (256 B) ‖ commitment (32 B).
fn process_instruction(
    _program_id: &Address,
    accounts: &mut [AccountView],
    instruction_data: &[u8],
) -> ProgramResult {
    let Some((proof, commitment)) = instruction_data.split_first_chunk::<256>() else {
        return Err(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData);
    };
    let commitment: [u8; 32] = commitment
        .try_into()
        .map_err(|_| ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
    let [holder, ..] = accounts else {
        return Err(ProgramError::NotEnoughAccountKeys);
    };
    if !holder.is_signer() {
        return Err(ProgramError::MissingRequiredSignature);
    }
    // The public inputs, in the circuit's order: commitment, the policy's two field
    // elements, holder.hi, holder.lo.
    let [packed, allowed] = policy(Clock::get()?.unix_timestamp);
    let [holder_hi, holder_lo] = halves(holder.address().as_array());
    let public_inputs = [commitment, packed, allowed, holder_hi, holder_lo];
    if verify(&Proof::from_le_bytes(proof), &public_inputs) {
        Ok(())
    } else {
        Err(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)
    }
}

/// An address as the circuit's `Hash`: `[hi, lo]`, each 16-byte half read as a
/// big-endian integer, as 32-byte little-endian field elements.
fn halves(bytes: &[u8; 32]) -> [[u8; 32]; 2] {
    let half = |b: &[u8]| {
        let mut out = [0u8; 32];
        out[..16].copy_from_slice(b);
        out[..16].reverse();
        out
    };
    [half(&bytes[..16]), half(&bytes[16..])]
}
```

Unlike 01, the instruction data isn't the whole statement: the program only takes the proof and
the commitment from the transaction. That, with the signer's address, is all that goes onchain:
the program stores nothing, and the identity and its salt stay with the holder and the issuer. See
[Where the public inputs come from](/docs/guides/how-verification-works#where-the-public-inputs-come-from).
</Step>

<Step>
### Build the policy onchain

A proof says *"this identity meets **this** policy"*. A holder who's too poor can make a perfectly
valid proof against a policy with no net-worth check. So the program never takes a policy from the
transaction: it builds its own, and the proof verifies only if the holder proved against exactly
that. The circuit stays the same for every verifier; each picks its rules by what it supplies.

The program can't link xark, so it builds the policy as the circuit takes it: the two field
elements from `xark inspect`, packed by hand. Append to `program/src/lib.rs`:

```rust title="program/src/lib.rs"
// The program's policy: 18 or older, worth at least 500,000, a national of DE, FR,
// GB or US, with a document valid today.
const MIN_AGE: i64 = 18;
const MIN_NET_WORTH: u64 = 500_000;
/// The allowed countries' positions in xark's `Country`, a bit each.
const ALLOWED: [u8; 32] = countries(&[56, 74, 76, 232]);

/// The program's `Policy` on the day holding the Unix time `unix`, as the circuit
/// takes it: `today | born_by << 17 | min_net_worth << 34` in one field element,
/// then `allowed` in its own.
fn policy(unix: i64) -> [[u8; 32]; 2] {
    let day = unix.div_euclid(86_400);
    let packed = u128::from(since_1900(day))
        | u128::from(born_by(day)) << 17
        | u128::from(MIN_NET_WORTH) << 34;
    let mut out = [0u8; 32];
    out[..16].copy_from_slice(&packed.to_le_bytes());
    [out, ALLOWED]
}

/// A `Bitfield<Country>` with bit `i` set for each position `i`.
const fn countries(positions: &[usize]) -> [u8; 32] {
    let mut out = [0u8; 32];
    let mut i = 0;
    while i < positions.len() {
        out[positions[i] / 8] |= 1 << (positions[i] % 8);
        i += 1;
    }
    out
}

/// A day counted from 1970-01-01 as the circuit's `Day` holds it: days since
/// 1900-01-01.
fn since_1900(day: i64) -> u32 {
    (day - days_from_civil(1900, 1, 1)) as u32
}

/// The latest birth date of someone `MIN_AGE` on `day`: the same day `MIN_AGE`
/// years earlier, or the 28th for a 29 February that year doesn't have.
fn born_by(day: i64) -> u32 {
    let (y, m, d) = civil_from_days(day);
    let y = y - MIN_AGE;
    let leap = (y % 4 == 0 && y % 100 != 0) || y % 400 == 0;
    let d = if m == 2 && d == 29 && !leap { 28 } else { d };
    since_1900(days_from_civil(y, m, d))
}

/// The civil date of a day count from 1970-01-01: Howard Hinnant's
/// `civil_from_days` (http://howardhinnant.github.io/date_algorithms.html).
fn civil_from_days(z: i64) -> (i64, i64, i64) {
    let z = z + 719_468;
    let era = z.div_euclid(146_097);
    let doe = z - era * 146_097;
    let yoe = (doe - doe / 1_460 + doe / 36_524 - doe / 146_096) / 365;
    let doy = doe - (365 * yoe + yoe / 4 - yoe / 100);
    let mp = (5 * doy + 2) / 153;
    let day = doy - (153 * mp + 2) / 5 + 1;
    let month = if mp < 10 { mp + 3 } else { mp - 9 };
    (yoe + era * 400 + i64::from(month <= 2), month, day)
}

/// Its inverse: Hinnant's `days_from_civil`.
fn days_from_civil(y: i64, m: i64, d: i64) -> i64 {
    let y = if m <= 2 { y - 1 } else { y };
    let era = y.div_euclid(400);
    let yoe = y - era * 400;
    let doy = (153 * (if m > 2 { m - 3 } else { m + 9 }) + 2) / 5 + d - 1;
    let doe = yoe * 365 + yoe / 4 - yoe / 100 + doy;
    era * 146_097 + doe - 719_468
}
```

```ts title="app/zkyc.test.ts"
// Send `instruction` in one transaction, paid by `payer`. Returns the compute
// units it used, or throws with the program's logs.
async function send(svm: LiteSVM, payer: KeyPairSigner, instruction: Instruction) {
  const tx = await pipe(
    createTransactionMessage({ version: 0 }),
    (m) => setTransactionMessageFeePayerSigner(payer, m),
    (m) => setTransactionMessageLifetimeUsingBlockhash({ blockhash: svm.latestBlockhash(), lastValidBlockHeight: 0n }, m),
    (m) => appendTransactionMessageInstruction(instruction, m),
    (m) => signTransactionMessageWithSigners(m),
  );
  const result = svm.sendTransaction(tx);
  svm.expireBlockhash();
  if (result instanceof FailedTransactionMetadata) {
    throw new Error(`transaction failed: ${result.err()}\n${result.meta().logs().join("\n")}`);
  }
  return result.computeUnitsConsumed();
}

// The program's instruction: signed by `holder`, with data proof ‖ commitment.
const verifyKyc = (program: Address, holder: KeyPairSigner, proof: Uint8Array, commitment: Field) => ({
  programAddress: program,
  accounts: [{ address: holder.address, role: AccountRole.READONLY_SIGNER, signer: holder }],
  data: new Uint8Array([...proof, ...commitment.toBytes()]),
});

// Set the VM's clock to noon UTC on `day`.
function setDay(svm: LiteSVM, day: string) {
  const clock = svm.getClock();
  clock.unixTimestamp = BigInt(Date.parse(`${day}T12:00:00Z`) / 1000);
  svm.setClock(clock);
}
```

```ts title="app/zkyc.test.ts"
test("the program accepts the proof for its holder, today, under its policy", async () => {
  const svm = new LiteSVM();
  const program = (await generateKeyPairSigner()).address;
  svm.addProgramFromFile(program, "../program/target/deploy/zkyc_program.so");
  const payer = await generateKeyPairSigner();
  svm.airdrop(payer.address, lamports(1_000_000_000n));
  setDay(svm, "2026-10-22");

  // The issuer's credential and its public commitment, and the holder's wallet.
  const id = credential();
  const commitment = Identity.commitment(id);
  const holder = await generateKeyPairSigner();
  const address = new Uint8Array(getAddressEncoder().encode(holder.address));

  const proof = prover.prove({ id, commitment, policy: programs("2026-10-22"), holder: address }).toBytes();
  const units = await send(svm, payer, verifyKyc(program, holder, proof, commitment));
  console.log(`verified onchain in ${units} CU`);

  // Someone who copies the proof can't submit it from their own wallet,
  const copycat = await generateKeyPairSigner();
  await expect(send(svm, payer, verifyKyc(program, copycat, proof, commitment))).rejects.toThrow(/invalid instruction data/);
  // or for someone else's commitment,
  const other = Identity.commitment(credential());
  await expect(send(svm, payer, verifyKyc(program, holder, proof, other))).rejects.toThrow(/invalid instruction data/);

  // A holder worth 100 proves against a policy with no net-worth check: a valid
  // proof, of a statement the program doesn't ask about.
  const poor = credential({ netWorth: 100 });
  const lax = { ...programs("2026-10-22"), minNetWorth: 0 };
  const laxProof = prover.prove({ id: poor, commitment: Identity.commitment(poor), policy: lax, holder: address }).toBytes();
  await expect(send(svm, payer, verifyKyc(program, holder, laxProof, Identity.commitment(poor)))).rejects.toThrow(/invalid instruction data/);

  // And tomorrow the program's policy has moved on.
  setDay(svm, "2026-10-23");
  await expect(send(svm, payer, verifyKyc(program, holder, proof, commitment))).rejects.toThrow(/invalid instruction data/);
});
```

## What you built

A holder proved to a Solana program that they pass its KYC policy, and the program learned only
that: not the birth date, the net worth, the nationality or the document number. 01's range proof
did the real work, on dates for the age and the expiry and on amounts for the net worth, and three
more tools kept the rest private:

- **Set membership.** `require_in` proved the nationality is in the program's `allowed` set without
  saying which country it is.
- **A salted commitment.** `require_eq(id.commitment(), commitment)` ties the private identity to
  the one the issuer committed to, so the prover can't choose it, and the salt makes that
  commitment, the only trace of the identity in the transaction, reveal nothing.
- **A bound signer.** `bind(holder)` ties the proof to one wallet, which the program fills in from
  the transaction's signer.

Underneath, **typed inputs** (`Text`, `Days`, `Choice<Country>`, `Bitfield<Country>`, `u64`) gave
the circuit, the tests and the TypeScript client the same types, **packing** fit `Policy` into two
public inputs, and **a policy from the verifier**, built from the program's constants and its
`Clock`, made the rules and "today" the program's, not the prover's.

A proof guarantees consistency, not honesty. It shows the identity inside the commitment meets the
policy; it says nothing about whether the commitment is true. This program accepts any commitment,
so anyone could commit to a 1900 birth date and a billion in net worth. A real one checks the
commitment against a registry its trusted issuers sign, or has the circuit verify the issuer's
signature with a [signature gadget](/docs/gadgets/signatures). A program serving several verifiers
would also keep each policy in an account rather than in constants, and pack it the same way.

Checking against a registry raises the next question: how does a circuit prove a value is in a list
of thousands without the list being a public input? The next tutorial answers it with a **Merkle
tree**: a token claim, where the list is one published root and your place in it a private path.
