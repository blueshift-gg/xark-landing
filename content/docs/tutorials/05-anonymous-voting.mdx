---
title: "05 · Anonymous voting"
description: Merkle membership plus a nullifier scoped to a poll. Voters on a list vote once per poll, through a relayer, and nobody learns who voted for what. A range proof keeps the choice among the options, and a Pinocchio program counts each vote.
---

In this tutorial you'll build anonymous voting. A poll's authority publishes one 32-byte root for a
list of registered voters. Each voter proves they're on the list and casts one vote, and a Solana
program counts it. Nobody learns who voted for what, or whether votes in two polls came from the
same person.

You have most of the pieces already. [04](/docs/tutorials/04-shielded-pool) put salted commitments
in [03](/docs/tutorials/03-token-claim)'s Merkle tree and spent each one once with a nullifier. A
voter list is that tree with nothing to withdraw. The new idea is a **scoped nullifier**: a note in
04 has one nullifier for its whole life, but a voter here has a different one in every poll. And
[01](/docs/tutorials/01-over-9000)'s [range proof](/docs/learn-zk/range-proofs) comes back, smaller:
the choice is proved to be one of the poll's options. By the end you'll have:

- a **circuit**, `vote`: a voter's identity, 03's tree of voters, and the circuit;
- a **TypeScript test** that builds the voter list and proves a vote with the generated client;
- a **Pinocchio program** that creates polls, verifies votes and counts each voter once per poll;
- an **end-to-end test**: voters vote through a relayer, a second vote and a replayed proof are
  refused, and the same voter votes again in a second poll.

## How it works

A voter picks two random field elements, a `nullifier_key` and a `trapdoor`, and keeps them: that's
their **identity**. They register its **commitment**, a Poseidon2 hash of both, with the poll's
authority, which puts every commitment in a Merkle tree and publishes the root. To vote, the voter
proves three things without saying which leaf is theirs:

1. their commitment is a leaf of the list (03's Merkle path);
2. the **nullifier** they reveal is the hash of their `nullifier_key` and the poll's ID;
3. their choice is one of the poll's options (01's range proof).

```text
identity   = (nullifier_key, trapdoor)            kept by the voter
commitment = commit([nullifier_key, trapdoor])    a leaf of the voter list
nullifier  = nullifier([nullifier_key, poll])     revealed by a vote in `poll`
```

The program keeps a receipt for each nullifier it counts, as 04 kept one for each nullifier it
paid, so a second vote in the same poll is refused. The authority sees commitments and the chain
sees nullifiers, and neither says anything about the other without the `nullifier_key`.

### Describe a voter

Replace `circuits/vote/src/lib.rs` with:

```rust title="circuits/vote/src/lib.rs"
use xark::prelude::*;
use xark_merkle::poseidon2::merkle_root;
#[cfg(not(xark))]
use xark_merkle::tree::{self, Tree};
#[cfg(not(xark))]
use xark_poseidon2::hash2;
use xark_poseidon2::{commit, nullifier};

/// A voter's secret: whoever knows it votes as this voter. Two random field
/// elements, kept by the voter; the voter list holds only their commitment.
#[input]
pub struct Identity {
    pub nullifier_key: Field,
    pub trapdoor: Field,
}

#[input]
impl Identity {
    /// What the voter registers: `commit(nullifier_key, trapdoor)`, a leaf of the
    /// voter list.
    pub fn commitment(&self) -> Field {
        commit([self.nullifier_key, self.trapdoor])
    }

    /// What a vote in `poll` reveals: `nullifier(nullifier_key, poll)`. One per
    /// voter per poll.
    pub fn nullifier(&self, poll: Field) -> Field {
        nullifier([self.nullifier_key, poll])
    }
}
```

An `Identity` is two random field elements, and `#[input] impl` puts its two hashes in the
TypeScript client, as `Claim.leaf` was in 03. The commitment is `xark_poseidon2::commit` of both
secrets; the nullifier is `xark_poseidon2::nullifier` of the key and the poll's ID. Both are
Poseidon2's sponge, started in a domain of its own, so no commitment is ever a nullifier, and
neither is a plain `hash` or a node of the tree, short of breaking Poseidon2. That's the domain
separation the voter list relies on below, built into the two functions.

That `poll` in the nullifier is the new idea, the **scoped nullifier**. 04's nullifier hashed the
note's secret alone: one per note, forever. This one is one per voter *per poll*:

- **One vote per poll.** In one poll, a voter's nullifier is always the same. A second vote, even
  with a fresh proof, reveals it again, and the program finds its receipt.
- **Unlinkable across polls.** In another poll it's another hash of the same key. Telling whether
  two nullifiers share a key means knowing the key, or breaking Poseidon2, so ten polls leave ten
  unrelated nullifiers.
- **Unlinkable to the list.** The commitment hashes the `trapdoor` too, which no nullifier does.
  The authority, which knows every commitment, can't match a nullifier to one.
</Step>

<Step>
### Add the voter list

The voter list is 03's tree, `xark-merkle`'s Poseidon2 tree, with commitments as its leaves.
Append 03's `list`, `Path` and its builder to `circuits/vote/src/lib.rs`, unchanged except for
their comments:

```rust title="circuits/vote/src/lib.rs"
/// Tree depth: up to 2^20 voters.
pub const DEPTH: usize = 20;

/// The empty leaf, ASCII `xarkzero`: no voter's commitment is this value, so
/// the padding after the list can't vote.
pub const EMPTY_LEAF: u64 = 0x7861_726b_7a65_726f;

/// The empty voter list, as an authority builds it in Rust: push each voter's
/// commitment, then take its `root()` and each voter's `path(i)`.
#[cfg(not(xark))]
pub fn list() -> Tree<DEPTH> {
    Tree::poseidon2(Field::from(EMPTY_LEAF))
}

/// A leaf's authentication path: its index, and its sibling at each height.
#[input]
pub struct Path {
    pub index: u32,
    pub siblings: [Field; 20],
}

#[input]
impl Path {
    /// The root of the tree with `leaf` at this path.
    pub fn root(&self, leaf: Field) -> Field {
        merkle_root(leaf, self.siblings, Field::from(self.index).to_bits::<DEPTH>())
    }

    /// The first leaf's path, in the empty list.
    #[cfg(not(xark))]
    pub fn first() -> Path {
        list().path(0).into()
    }

    /// For the next free leaf's path, the root of the list so far.
    #[cfg(not(xark))]
    pub fn list_root(&self) -> Field {
        self.root(Field::from(EMPTY_LEAF))
    }

    /// The next free leaf's path, once `leaf` is added at this one.
    #[cfg(not(xark))]
    pub fn next(self, leaf: Field) -> Path {
        assert!((self.index as usize) < (1 << DEPTH) - 1, "the list is full");
        let index = self.index + 1;
        let empty = list();
        let mut siblings = self.siblings;
        let mut node = leaf;
        for (i, sibling) in siblings.iter_mut().enumerate() {
            let (mine, next) = (self.index >> i, index >> i);
            let old = *sibling;
            if next != mine {
                // A new subtree: its left sibling is this leaf's, its right is empty.
                *sibling = if next & 1 == 1 { node } else { empty.empty_subtrees()[i] };
            }
            node = node_of(mine, node, old);
        }
        Path { index, siblings }
    }

    /// This path, once `leaf` is added at `later`, a later leaf's path.
    #[cfg(not(xark))]
    pub fn update(self, later: Path, leaf: Field) -> Path {
        assert!(later.index > self.index, "`later` must be a later leaf's path");
        let mut siblings = self.siblings;
        let mut node = leaf;
        for (i, (sibling, beside)) in siblings.iter_mut().zip(later.siblings).enumerate() {
            let at = later.index >> i;
            if at == self.index >> i ^ 1 {
                *sibling = node;
            }
            node = node_of(at, node, beside);
        }
        Path { index: self.index, siblings }
    }
}

/// A path out of a `Tree`, as the circuit takes it.
#[cfg(not(xark))]
impl From<tree::Path<DEPTH>> for Path {
    fn from(path: tree::Path<DEPTH>) -> Path {
        Path { index: path.index as u32, siblings: path.siblings }
    }
}

/// The parent of `node`, at position `at` in its level, and its `sibling`.
#[cfg(not(xark))]
fn node_of(at: u32, node: Field, sibling: Field) -> Field {
    if at & 1 == 0 { hash2(node, sibling) } else { hash2(sibling, node) }
}
```

[03's tree step](/docs/tutorials/03-token-claim#write-the-tree) explains it: `root` is the one
function the circuit calls, `xark_merkle::poseidon2::merkle_root`, which folds the path with a
`cond_swap` and a Poseidon2 `hash2` per level. `list` is the whole list on the host, a
`xark_merkle::tree::Tree`, for a Rust authority that holds every commitment. `first`, `next`,
`update` and `list_root` keep one path at a time, without the tree, which is what the authority
and the voters run from TypeScript. A node is the compression `hash2`, and a commitment and a
nullifier each have their own domain of the sponge, so at a fixed depth no node passes for a
voter, and no nullifier for either. The empty leaf is a constant no commitment hashes to, so the
padding after the list can't vote.
</Step>

<Step>
### Write the circuit and its tests

A vote's public statement is its `Ballot`: the list's root, the poll, the nullifier, the number of
options and the choice. Append it, the circuit and three tests:

```rust title="circuits/vote/src/lib.rs"
/// What the program checks a vote against: the public inputs, in this order.
#[input]
pub struct Ballot {
    pub root: Field,
    pub poll: Field,
    pub nullifier: Field,
    pub options: u8,
    pub choice: u8,
}

#[input]
impl Ballot {
    /// `identity`, at `path`, voting `choice` of `options` in `poll`.
    pub fn of(identity: Identity, path: Path, poll: Field, options: u8, choice: u8) -> Ballot {
        Ballot {
            root: path.root(identity.commitment()),
            poll,
            nullifier: identity.nullifier(poll),
            options,
            choice,
        }
    }
}

#[circuit]
pub fn vote(identity: Private<Identity>, path: Private<Path>, ballot: Public<Ballot>) {
    require_eq(path.root(identity.commitment()), ballot.root);
    require_eq(identity.nullifier(ballot.poll), ballot.nullifier);
    require(ballot.choice < ballot.options);
}

#[cfg(test)]
mod tests {
    use super::*;

    fn identity(n: u64) -> Identity {
        Identity { nullifier_key: Field::from(n), trapdoor: Field::from(n + 1_000) }
    }

    /// The list's root and every voter's path, as the authority builds them.
    fn of(voters: &[Identity]) -> (Field, Vec<Path>) {
        let mut tree = list();
        tree.extend(voters.iter().map(Identity::commitment));
        let paths = (0..voters.len()).map(|i| tree.path(i).into()).collect();
        (tree.root(), paths)
    }

    /// One path at a time (`first`, `next`, `update`) agrees with the `Tree`.
    #[test]
    fn one_path_at_a_time_agrees_with_the_tree() {
        let leaves: Vec<Field> = (1..=11).map(|n| identity(n).commitment()).collect();
        let mut tree = list();
        let mut tip = Path::first(); // the next free leaf's path
        let mut paths: Vec<Path> = Vec::new();
        assert_eq!(tip.list_root(), tree.root());
        for &leaf in &leaves {
            paths = paths.into_iter().map(|p| p.update(tip, leaf)).collect();
            paths.push(tip);
            tip = tip.next(leaf);
            tree.push(leaf);
            assert_eq!(tip.list_root(), tree.root());
            for (i, path) in paths.iter().chain([&tip]).enumerate() {
                let want = tree.path(i);
                assert_eq!((path.index as usize, path.siblings), (want.index, want.siblings));
            }
        }
    }

    #[test]
    fn one_nullifier_per_voter_per_poll() {
        let (a, b) = (identity(1), identity(2));
        let (one, two) = (Field::from(1u64), Field::from(2u64));
        assert_ne!(a.nullifier(one), a.nullifier(two)); // another poll
        assert_ne!(a.nullifier(one), b.nullifier(one)); // another voter
        assert_ne!(a.nullifier(one), a.commitment()); // not the leaf
    }

    #[test]
    fn every_voter_votes_for_any_option() {
        let voters = [identity(1), identity(2), identity(3)];
        let (root, paths) = of(&voters);
        for (path, v) in paths.iter().zip(voters) {
            for choice in 0..3 {
                let ballot = Ballot::of(v, *path, Field::from(7u64), 3, choice);
                assert_eq!(ballot.root, root);
                vote(v, *path, ballot).unwrap();
            }
        }
    }

    #[test]
    fn nothing_else_votes() {
        let voters = [identity(1), identity(2), identity(3)];
        let (root, paths) = of(&voters);
        let poll = Field::from(7u64);
        let ok = Ballot::of(voters[0], paths[0], poll, 3, 1);
        // Past the poll's options, which are 0, 1 and 2.
        assert!(vote(voters[0], paths[0], Ballot { choice: 3, ..ok }).is_err());
        assert!(vote(voters[0], paths[0], Ballot { choice: 255, ..ok }).is_err());
        // Someone not on the list, at a voter's path.
        let stranger = identity(9);
        let theirs = Ballot::of(stranger, paths[0], poll, 3, 1);
        assert!(vote(stranger, paths[0], Ballot { root, ..theirs }).is_err());
        // Another voter's nullifier, or this voter's in another poll.
        let nullifier = voters[1].nullifier(poll);
        assert!(vote(voters[0], paths[0], Ballot { nullifier, ..ok }).is_err());
        let nullifier = voters[0].nullifier(Field::from(8u64));
        assert!(vote(voters[0], paths[0], Ballot { nullifier, ..ok }).is_err());
    }
}
```

The circuit is three lines, one per thing the voter proves. `identity` and `path` are private: the
path's index says which voter this is, so it never leaves the voter's device. The `ballot` is
public, and the program will supply it.

The last line is the [range proof](/docs/learn-zk/range-proofs). `options` and `choice` are `u8`s,
so each is split into 8 bits as it's read, and both are below 2^8. `choice < options` is then 01's
comparison, between two values rather than against a constant: xark range-proves
`choice − options + 2^8` in 8 bits, which fits exactly when `choice < options`. A choice of 3 in a
three-option poll leaves nothing to prove. Without the bits, a "negative" difference would wrap
around the field to a huge value that passes for a positive one, and `p − 1` would be "less than"
anything: the bits are what give `<` its meaning.

`options` is a public input rather than a constant in the circuit, so one circuit, one key and
one verifier serve polls of any size. The program fills it in from the poll's account, and never
checks the choice itself.

There's no `bind`, unlike 04's addresses: `root` and `nullifier` are compared, `poll` is hashed,
and `options` and `choice` are range-checked and ordered, so every public input is already in a
constraint.

`of` builds a list from the `Tree`: it pushes each voter's commitment and reads back the root and
each voter's path. The first test checks the one-path-at-a-time builder against it, path for path,
over eleven voters. The second checks the nullifier's scope: another poll or another voter gives
another nullifier, and neither is the commitment. The third has every voter vote for every option.
The fourth refuses everything else: a choice past the options, a stranger at a voter's path,
another voter's nullifier and the same voter's nullifier from another poll.
</Step>

```ts title="tests/anonymous-voting.test.ts"
import { circuit } from "@blueshift-gg/xark/testing";
import { Ballot, Field, Identity, Path, Vote, type PathValue, type VoteProver } from "../clients/vote/ts/vote";

let prover: VoteProver;
beforeAll(async () => {
  // The circuit's build output and its keys. A web app would serve these and
  // pass their bytes instead.
  prover = await Vote.prover(circuit("vote"), { digest: Vote.digest });
});

/** A new voter: two random field elements, kept by the voter. */
const newIdentity = (): Identity => ({
  nullifierKey: new Field(crypto.getRandomValues(new Uint8Array(31))),
  trapdoor: new Field(crypto.getRandomValues(new Uint8Array(31))),
});

/** The authority's voter list: its root, and each voter's path. */
function list(voters: Identity[]): { root: Field; paths: PathValue[] } {
  let tip = Path.first(); // the next free leaf's path
  let paths: PathValue[] = [];
  for (const voter of voters) {
    const leaf = Identity.commitment(voter);
    paths = paths.map((p) => p.update(tip, leaf));
    paths.push(tip);
    tip = tip.next(leaf);
  }
  return { root: tip.listRoot(), paths };
}

test("a voter on the list has a proof, and a new nullifier in each poll", async () => {
  const voters = [newIdentity(), newIdentity(), newIdentity()];
  const { root, paths } = list(voters);
  const ballot = Ballot.of(voters[1], paths[1], 1n, 3, 2);
  expect(ballot.root).toEqual(root);

  const proof = prover.prove({ identity: voters[1], path: paths[1], ballot });

  // The verifier sees the ballot, and nothing of the identity or the path.
  const verifier = await Vote.verifier(circuit("vote").verifyingKey);
  expect(verifier.verify(proof.toBytes(), { ballot })).toBe(true);
  expect(verifier.verify(proof.toBytes(), { ballot: { ...ballot, choice: 0 } })).toBe(false);

  // The same voter in poll 2 reveals another nullifier.
  expect(Ballot.of(voters[1], paths[1], 2n, 3, 2).nullifier).not.toEqual(ballot.nullifier);
});

test("nothing else has a proof", () => {
  const voters = [newIdentity(), newIdentity()];
  const { root, paths } = list(voters);
  // A choice past the poll's three options.
  const past = Ballot.of(voters[0], paths[0], 1n, 3, 3);
  expect(() => prover.prove({ identity: voters[0], path: paths[0], ballot: past })).toThrow(/ballot\.choice < ballot\.options/);
  // An identity that never registered, at a voter's path.
  const stranger = newIdentity();
  const theirs = { ...Ballot.of(stranger, paths[0], 1n, 3, 0), root };
  expect(() => prover.prove({ identity: stranger, path: paths[0], ballot: theirs })).toThrow(/path\.root\(identity\.commitment\(\)\)/);
});
```

`newIdentity` is what a voter's wallet does once: two random secrets of 31 bytes each, so each is
below the field's modulus. `list` is 03's, over commitments: the authority adds each voter's
commitment at the tip and publishes `tip.listRoot()`. It never sees an identity, only its
commitment.

### Lay out the accounts

The program owns two kinds of account, each at a PDA:

| PDA | Seeds | Size |
|---|---|--:|
| `poll` | `["poll", poll_id as u64 LE]` | 112 |
| `receipt` | `["voted", poll, nullifier]` | 0 |

The poll is seeded by its ID alone, not by its authority as well, so no two polls share an ID.
That's what keeps the nullifier scoped: two polls with one ID would share each voter's nullifier,
and anyone could link a voter's votes in the two. A receipt holds nothing; that it exists is
the vote.

The account checks are [04's](/docs/tutorials/04-shielded-pool#check-every-account), which says
what each one prevents, and so is `log`. Create `programs/anonymous-voting/src/helpers.rs`:

```rust title="programs/anonymous-voting/src/state.rs"
use pinocchio::{AccountView, Address, ProgramResult, error::ProgramError};

use crate::helpers::writable;

pub const POLL_SEED: &[u8] = b"poll";
pub const VOTED_SEED: &[u8] = b"voted";

/// The most options a poll can count. The circuit takes any `u8`; the tally's
/// size is the program's.
pub const MAX_OPTIONS: usize = 8;

/// A poll's account, 112 bytes: tag, bump, `options`, 5 reserved bytes, then
/// `id` (u64 LE), the voter list's `root`, and a tally per option (u64 LE).
#[repr(C)]
pub struct Poll {
    pub tag: u8,
    pub bump: u8,
    pub options: u8,
    reserved: [u8; 5],
    pub id: [u8; 8],
    pub root: [u8; 32],
    tally: [[u8; 8]; MAX_OPTIONS],
}

const _: () = assert!(Poll::LEN == 112);

impl Poll {
    pub const LEN: usize = size_of::<Self>();
    pub const TAG: u8 = 1;

    /// The poll `data` holds, if it is a poll's size and tag.
    pub fn load(data: &[u8]) -> Result<&Self, ProgramError> {
        if data.len() != Self::LEN || data[0] != Self::TAG {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidAccountData);
        }
        // SAFETY: `Poll` is all byte arrays (alignment 1, any bit pattern valid),
        // and `data` is exactly its size.
        Ok(unsafe { &*data.as_ptr().cast::<Self>() })
    }

    /// As `load`, to write.
    pub fn load_mut(data: &mut [u8]) -> Result<&mut Self, ProgramError> {
        if data.len() != Self::LEN || data[0] != Self::TAG {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidAccountData);
        }
        // SAFETY: as above.
        Ok(unsafe { &mut *data.as_mut_ptr().cast::<Self>() })
    }

    /// Write a new poll into a freshly created account, which is zeroed: every
    /// tally starts at zero.
    pub fn init(
        data: &mut [u8],
        id: [u8; 8],
        root: &[u8; 32],
        options: u8,
        bump: u8,
    ) -> ProgramResult {
        if data.len() != Self::LEN {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidAccountData);
        }
        // SAFETY: as above.
        let poll = unsafe { &mut *data.as_mut_ptr().cast::<Self>() };
        poll.tag = Self::TAG;
        poll.bump = bump;
        poll.options = options;
        poll.id = id;
        poll.root = *root;
        Ok(())
    }

    /// A poll account: writable, owned by this program, a poll's size and tag, at
    /// the address its own id and stored bump derive.
    pub fn check(account: &AccountView) -> ProgramResult {
        writable(account)?;
        if !account.owned_by(&crate::ID) {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidAccountOwner);
        }
        let data = account.try_borrow()?;
        let poll = Self::load(&data)?;
        let address = Address::derive_address(&[POLL_SEED, &poll.id], Some(poll.bump), &crate::ID);
        if account.address() != &address {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidSeeds);
        }
        Ok(())
    }

    /// Count one vote for `choice`.
    pub fn count(&mut self, choice: u8) -> ProgramResult {
        let tally = self
            .tally
            .get_mut(usize::from(choice))
            .ok_or(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
        let votes = u64::from_le_bytes(*tally)
            .checked_add(1)
            .ok_or(ProgramError::ArithmeticOverflow)?;
        *tally = votes.to_le_bytes();
        Ok(())
    }
}
```

A poll is plain bytes, as 04's pool was, and `load` reads it in place. The reserved bytes put `id`
at offset 8, so the tally starts at 48, where the test will read it. The tally has room for 8
options; the circuit would take up to 255. `count` adds one vote with checked arithmetic, and the
`get_mut` would refuse a choice past the tally, though the proof never lets one through.

`Poll::check` is 04's `Pool::check`, and each of its checks closes a hole:

- **Writable**: the vote writes the tally, so a read-only poll fails up front, with `Immutable`.
- **Owned by this program**: only this program can write an account it owns, so the root and the
  options are a real poll's, not an account someone wrote with the root of a list of their own.
- **A poll's size and tag**, so a receipt can't pass for a poll.
- **At the address its own ID and stored bump derive**, so the account is the poll it says it is.
  The bump is the one `create_poll` stored, so the check is one hash, not a search.
</Step>

```rust title="programs/anonymous-voting/src/instructions/create_poll.rs"
use pinocchio::{
    AccountView, Address, ProgramResult,
    cpi::{Seed, Signer},
    error::ProgramError,
};

use crate::{
    helpers::{is_uninitialized, system_program, writable, writable_signer},
    state::{MAX_OPTIONS, POLL_SEED, Poll},
};

/// Accounts: the authority (signs, pays the rent), the poll (the PDA, not yet
/// created), the system program.
pub struct CreatePollAccounts<'a> {
    pub authority: &'a AccountView,
    pub poll: &'a mut AccountView,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<&'a mut [AccountView]> for CreatePollAccounts<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from(accounts: &'a mut [AccountView]) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let [authority, poll, system, ..] = accounts else {
            return Err(ProgramError::NotEnoughAccountKeys);
        };
        writable_signer(authority)?;
        writable(poll)?;
        system_program(system)?;
        Ok(Self { authority, poll })
    }
}

/// Data: poll_id (u64 LE) ‖ root (32) ‖ options (1).
pub struct CreatePollInstructionData<'a> {
    pub poll_id: [u8; 8],
    pub root: &'a [u8; 32],
    pub options: u8,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<&'a [u8]> for CreatePollInstructionData<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from(data: &'a [u8]) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let (poll_id, data) = data
            .split_first_chunk::<8>()
            .ok_or(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
        let (root, data) = data
            .split_first_chunk::<32>()
            .ok_or(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
        let [options] = *data else {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData);
        };
        // At least a real choice, and no more than the tally has room for.
        if !(2..=MAX_OPTIONS as u8).contains(&options) {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidArgument);
        }
        Ok(Self {
            poll_id: *poll_id,
            root,
            options,
        })
    }
}

/// Create poll `poll_id` over the voter list rooted at `root`, with `options`
/// options, each tally at zero.
pub struct CreatePoll<'a> {
    pub accounts: CreatePollAccounts<'a>,
    pub data: CreatePollInstructionData<'a>,
    pub bump: u8,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<(&'a [u8], &'a mut [AccountView])> for CreatePoll<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from((data, accounts): (&'a [u8], &'a mut [AccountView])) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let accounts = CreatePollAccounts::try_from(accounts)?;
        let data = CreatePollInstructionData::try_from(data)?;
        // One poll per id, at the canonical bump.
        let (address, bump) =
            Address::find_program_address(&[POLL_SEED, &data.poll_id], &crate::ID);
        if accounts.poll.address() != &address {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidSeeds);
        }
        if !is_uninitialized(accounts.poll) {
            return Err(ProgramError::AccountAlreadyInitialized);
        }
        Ok(Self {
            accounts,
            data,
            bump,
        })
    }
}

impl<'a> CreatePoll<'a> {
    pub const DISCRIMINATOR: &'a u8 = &0;

    pub fn process(&mut self) -> ProgramResult {
        let bump = [self.bump];
        let seeds = [
            Seed::from(POLL_SEED),
            Seed::from(&self.data.poll_id),
            Seed::from(&bump),
        ];
        pinocchio_system::create_account_with_minimum_balance_signed(
            self.accounts.poll,
            Poll::LEN,
            &crate::ID,
            self.accounts.authority,
            None,
            &[Signer::from(&seeds)],
        )?;
        let mut data = self.accounts.poll.try_borrow_mut()?;
        Poll::init(
            &mut data,
            self.data.poll_id,
            self.data.root,
            self.data.options,
            self.bump,
        )
    }
}
```

What each check prevents:

- **The authority is a writable signer**: it pays the poll's rent, so no one spends someone else's
  SOL.
- **The poll is writable, and the system program is the system program**, so a transaction missing
  either fails before the CPI, with `Immutable` or `IncorrectProgramId`.
- **The data is exactly 41 bytes**, so a short or long instruction is refused before anything
  reads it.
- **`options` is from 2 to 8**: one option is no choice, and the tally has room for eight.
- **The poll is the ID's PDA at its canonical bump.** Another bump would give a second poll with
  the same ID, and so with the same nullifiers.
- **The poll doesn't exist yet**, so no one can reset a live poll's tally or swap its root.

Anyone can create a poll, and the first to take an ID has it. An authority should pick a random
`u64`, and announce the poll's address with its root.
</Step>

### Count a vote

Create `programs/anonymous-voting/src/instructions/vote.rs`:

```rust title="programs/anonymous-voting/src/instructions/vote.rs"
use pinocchio::{
    AccountView, Address, ProgramResult,
    cpi::{Seed, Signer},
    error::ProgramError,
};
use vote_verifier::{Ballot, Proof, PublicInputs, fields, verify};

use crate::{
    VotingError,
    helpers::{is_uninitialized, system_program, writable, writable_signer},
    state::{Poll, VOTED_SEED},
};

/// Accounts: the payer (any signer: a relayer), the poll, the receipt (the PDA,
/// not yet created), the system program.
pub struct VoteAccounts<'a> {
    pub payer: &'a AccountView,
    pub poll: &'a mut AccountView,
    pub receipt: &'a mut AccountView,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<&'a mut [AccountView]> for VoteAccounts<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from(accounts: &'a mut [AccountView]) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let [payer, poll, receipt, system, ..] = accounts else {
            return Err(ProgramError::NotEnoughAccountKeys);
        };
        // Anyone pays: the proof doesn't name the payer, so a relayer can submit
        // it without the voter's wallet appearing.
        writable_signer(payer)?;
        Poll::check(poll)?;
        writable(receipt)?;
        system_program(system)?;
        Ok(Self {
            payer,
            poll,
            receipt,
        })
    }
}

/// Data: proof (256) ‖ nullifier (32) ‖ choice (1).
pub struct VoteInstructionData<'a> {
    pub proof: &'a [u8; 256],
    pub nullifier: &'a [u8; 32],
    pub choice: u8,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<&'a [u8]> for VoteInstructionData<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from(data: &'a [u8]) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let (proof, data) = data
            .split_first_chunk::<256>()
            .ok_or(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
        let (nullifier, data) = data
            .split_first_chunk::<32>()
            .ok_or(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData)?;
        let [choice] = *data else {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidInstructionData);
        };
        Ok(Self {
            proof,
            nullifier,
            choice,
        })
    }
}

/// Count one vote for `choice`, if the proof says a voter on the poll's list made
/// it and `nullifier` is theirs in this poll. The `receipt` is created here: if
/// it exists, this voter has voted.
pub struct Vote<'a> {
    pub accounts: VoteAccounts<'a>,
    pub data: VoteInstructionData<'a>,
    pub receipt_bump: u8,
}

impl<'a> TryFrom<(&'a [u8], &'a mut [AccountView])> for Vote<'a> {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from((data, accounts): (&'a [u8], &'a mut [AccountView])) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        let accounts = VoteAccounts::try_from(accounts)?;
        let data = VoteInstructionData::try_from(data)?;
        // The receipt, at its canonical bump: another bump would be another address
        // for the same nullifier, to vote again at.
        let seeds = [VOTED_SEED, accounts.poll.address().as_ref(), data.nullifier];
        let (address, receipt_bump) = Address::find_program_address(&seeds, &crate::ID);
        if accounts.receipt.address() != &address {
            return Err(ProgramError::InvalidSeeds);
        }
        if !is_uninitialized(accounts.receipt) {
            return Err(VotingError::AlreadyVoted.into());
        }
        Ok(Self {
            accounts,
            data,
            receipt_bump,
        })
    }
}

impl<'a> Vote<'a> {
    pub const DISCRIMINATOR: &'a u8 = &1;

    pub fn process(&mut self) -> ProgramResult {
        let inputs = {
            let data = self.accounts.poll.try_borrow()?;
            public_inputs(Poll::load(&data)?, self.data.nullifier, self.data.choice)
        };
        if !verify(&Proof::from_le_bytes(self.data.proof), inputs) {
            return Err(VotingError::InvalidProof.into());
        }

        // Record the vote: create the nullifier's receipt, paid by the payer.
        let bump = [self.receipt_bump];
        let seeds = [
            Seed::from(VOTED_SEED),
            Seed::from(self.accounts.poll.address().as_ref()),
            Seed::from(self.data.nullifier),
            Seed::from(&bump),
        ];
        pinocchio_system::create_account_with_minimum_balance_signed(
            self.accounts.receipt,
            0,
            &crate::ID,
            self.accounts.payer,
            None,
            &[Signer::from(&seeds)],
        )?;

        Poll::load_mut(&mut self.accounts.poll.try_borrow_mut()?)?.count(self.data.choice)
    }
}

/// The circuit's public `Ballot`: the root, the id and the options from the poll,
/// the nullifier and the choice from the instruction.
fn public_inputs(poll: &Poll, nullifier: &[u8; 32], choice: u8) -> PublicInputs {
    PublicInputs {
        ballot: Ballot {
            root: poll.root,
            poll: fields::uint(u64::from_le_bytes(poll.id).into()),
            nullifier: *nullifier,
            options: poll.options,
            choice,
        },
    }
}
```

`public_inputs` fills in the circuit's `Ballot` by name, with the generated `PublicInputs`. The
root, the ID and the options come from the poll's account, and the nullifier and the choice from
the instruction. The poll's ID is a `u64`, and `fields::uint` makes it the field element the
circuit's `poll` is. The verifier crate does the rest: it puts the fields in the order `xark
inspect` printed and packs `options` and `choice` into one field element, as `#[input]` packed
them. A proof made for another poll, another list or another choice fails `verify`, with `InvalidProof`.
The proof stays a reference into the instruction data, as in 03: a copy on the stack, beside the
verifier's own, would crowd the 4 KiB frame a Solana program gets.

The other checks:

- **The payer is a writable signer**: it pays the receipt's rent and the fee, so no one spends
  someone else's SOL.
- **`Poll::check`**, so the root, the ID and the options the proof is checked against are a real
  poll's.
- **The receipt is the nullifier's PDA in this poll, at its canonical bump.** It's seeded by the
  poll and the nullifier, so a voter can't pass a fresh account each time. The bump is the one
  `find_program_address` finds, not one the client sends, or the same nullifier would have a
  receipt at each of the other bumps too.
- **The receipt doesn't exist yet.** If it does, this voter has voted in this poll:
  `AlreadyVoted`. Lamports sent to its address beforehand don't block a vote; creation tops them
  up.
- **The data is exactly 289 bytes**, so the proof the verifier reads is the 256 bytes sent.
- **The system program**, which the CPI that creates the receipt calls.

The verifier refuses a public input that isn't a canonical field element, so `nullifier + p`,
the same number in the field, can't vote again at another receipt's address.

**The signer isn't in the proof, on purpose.** 03 bound the claimant: the program took it from the
signer, so only the claimant's wallet could claim. A vote that carried the voter's wallet would
name the voter. So any signer pays, and in the test it's a **relayer**, a wallet that submits
other people's votes, so the voter's wallet never appears onchain. The relayer can't change a
vote: everything it could touch is a public input, and a proof sent for another choice or another
poll doesn't verify. It can only drop the vote, and the voter then submits elsewhere. It can
re-randomize the proof, as anyone can a Groth16 proof, but the copy proves the same ballot, with
the same nullifier, and the receipt counts it once.
</Step>

### Name the errors

Every other check returns a built-in Solana error: `NotEnoughAccountKeys`,
`InvalidInstructionData`, `MissingRequiredSignature`, `Immutable`, `IncorrectProgramId`,
`InvalidAccountOwner`, `InvalidAccountData`, `InvalidSeeds`, `AccountAlreadyInitialized`,

`programs/anonymous-voting/src/errors.rs` with:

```rust title="programs/anonymous-voting/src/errors.rs"
use pinocchio::error::{ProgramError, ToStr};

/// The program's own errors, as `ProgramError::Custom` codes.
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
#[repr(u32)]
pub enum VotingError {
    InvalidProof = 0,
    AlreadyVoted,
}

impl From<VotingError> for ProgramError {
    fn from(e: VotingError) -> Self {
        ProgramError::Custom(e as u32)
    }
}

impl TryFrom<u32> for VotingError {
    type Error = ProgramError;

    fn try_from(code: u32) -> Result<Self, Self::Error> {
        match code {
            0 => Ok(Self::InvalidProof),
            1 => Ok(Self::AlreadyVoted),
            _ => Err(ProgramError::InvalidArgument),
        }
    }
}

/// What the entrypoint logs: the name, then what it means.
impl ToStr for VotingError {
    fn to_str(&self) -> &'static str {
        match self {
            Self::InvalidProof => "InvalidProof: invalid proof",
            Self::AlreadyVoted => "AlreadyVoted: this nullifier has voted in this poll",
        }
    }
}
```

`InvalidProof` is `0` and `AlreadyVoted` is `1`, and the entrypoint logs each one's name.
</Step>

const CREATE_POLL = 0;
const VOTE = 1;

// A u64, little-endian, as the program and the PDA seeds read it.
function u64le(x: bigint) {

```ts title="tests/anonymous-voting.test.ts"
/** `create_poll`: poll `id` over the list rooted at `root`, with `options` options. */
async function createPoll(svm: LiteSVM, authority: KeyPairSigner, id: bigint, root: Field, options: number) {
  const [poll] = await getProgramDerivedAddress({ programAddress: program, seeds: ["poll", u64le(id)] });
  await send(svm, authority, {
    programAddress: program,
    accounts: [signer(authority), writable(poll), readonly(system)],
    data: new Uint8Array([CREATE_POLL, ...u64le(id), ...root.toBytes(), options]),
  });
  return poll;
}

/** `vote`: `choice` in `poll`, with its proof and nullifier, paid by `payer`. */
async function vote(poll: Address, payer: KeyPairSigner, proof: Uint8Array, nullifier: Field, choice: number): Promise<Instruction> {
  const [receipt] = await getProgramDerivedAddress({ programAddress: program, seeds: ["voted", bytes(poll), nullifier.toBytes()] });
  return {
    programAddress: program,
    accounts: [signer(payer), writable(poll), writable(receipt), readonly(system)],
    data: new Uint8Array([VOTE, ...proof, ...nullifier.toBytes(), choice]),
  };
}

/** The poll's tally, one count per option: `options` is byte 2, the tally starts at 48. */
function tally(svm: LiteSVM, poll: Address): bigint[] {
  const account = svm.getAccount(poll);
  if (!account.exists) throw new Error("no poll");
  const view = new DataView(account.data.buffer, account.data.byteOffset);
  return Array.from({ length: account.data[2] }, (_, i) => view.getBigUint64(48 + 8 * i, true));
}
```

Finally, the test. Four voters register and the authority opens a poll. Three vote through the
relayer, and then everything that shouldn't count is tried:

```ts title="tests/anonymous-voting.test.ts"
test("each voter on the list votes once per poll, through a relayer", async () => {
  const svm = vm({ [program]: deployed("anonymous-voting") });
  const authority = await funded(svm);
  // The relayer signs and pays for every vote, so no voter's wallet appears.
  const relayer = await funded(svm);

  // Four voters register; the authority opens poll 1, with three options, over their list.
  const voters = [newIdentity(), newIdentity(), newIdentity(), newIdentity()];
  const { root, paths } = list(voters);
  const poll = await createPoll(svm, authority, 1n, root, 3);

  // Voter `i`'s proof for `choice` in poll `id`, and the nullifier it reveals.
  const ballot = (i: number, id: bigint, choice: number) => {
    const b = Ballot.of(voters[i], paths[i], id, 3, choice);
    const proof = prover.prove({ identity: voters[i], path: paths[i], ballot: b });
    return { proof: proof.toBytes(), nullifier: b.nullifier };
  };

  // Three of them vote: voter 0 for option 0, voters 1 and 2 for option 2.
  for (const [i, choice] of [[0, 0], [1, 2], [2, 2]]) {
    const { proof, nullifier } = ballot(i, 1n, choice);
    const result = await send(svm, relayer, await vote(poll, relayer, proof, nullifier, choice));
    console.log(`vote counted in ${result.computeUnitsConsumed()} CU`);
  }
  expect(tally(svm, poll)).toEqual([1n, 0n, 2n]);

  // Voter 0 again, with a fresh proof: the same nullifier, whose receipt exists.
  const again = ballot(0, 1n, 1);
  await expect(send(svm, relayer, await vote(poll, relayer, again.proof, again.nullifier, 1))).rejects.toThrow(/AlreadyVoted/);

  // Voter 3's proof for option 1, replayed for option 0: the program fills in the
  // choice, and the proof fails. So does the same proof sent to poll 2.
  const three = ballot(3, 1n, 1);
  await expect(send(svm, relayer, await vote(poll, relayer, three.proof, three.nullifier, 0))).rejects.toThrow(/InvalidProof/);
  const second = await createPoll(svm, authority, 2n, root, 3);
  await expect(send(svm, relayer, await vote(second, relayer, three.proof, three.nullifier, 1))).rejects.toThrow(/InvalidProof/);

  // Voter 0 votes in poll 2, with a nullifier unlike their poll-1 one, and it counts.
  const other = ballot(0, 2n, 1);
  expect(other.nullifier).not.toEqual(again.nullifier);
  await send(svm, relayer, await vote(second, relayer, other.proof, other.nullifier, 1));
  expect(tally(svm, second)).toEqual([0n, 1n, 0n]);
});
```

What the chain does show:

- **The tally.** Each vote's choice is in its transaction, and the running count is public at
  every slot. Only who voted is hidden. Hiding the choices until the poll ends needs encrypted
  ballots, which this tutorial doesn't build.
- **Each vote's timing.** Every vote lands at a known slot. If only one voter on the list was
  awake at 3 a.m., a vote at 3 a.m. is theirs. The anonymity set is the list, and in practice only
  the voters who act like everyone else.

## What you built

A poll counted votes from a list of voters, once each, without learning who cast which. The new
ideas:

- **A scoped nullifier.** `nullifier([nullifier_key, poll])` is the same for every vote of one voter
  in one poll, so the program's receipt counts them once, and unrelated across polls, so voting in
  many leaves nothing to link.
- **Merkle membership, again.** A voter's commitment is a leaf of 03's tree, and the path to it
  stays on their device, as 04's did.
- **A range proof on the choice.** `choice < options` keeps the vote among the poll's options, with
  `options` a public input that shares a field element with `choice`, so one circuit serves polls
  of any size.
- **An unbound signer.** The proof names no wallet, so a relayer submits the vote and pays for it,
  and can't change it.
- **A Pinocchio program** around the generated verifier crate: a PDA per poll and per vote, and
  every account check written out.

The list is the authority's. It knows who each commitment belongs to, and could register voters
who don't exist: the circuit proves membership in the list, not that the list is fair. A poll
stores one root, so registering later means a new list and a new poll; a voter's identity carries
over, and their nullifiers stay unlinkable.

<Callout type="warn" title="Before you deploy">
`xark add program` made a single-party key: anyone who kept its randomness could forge a proof for any
ballot, and vote as often as they like. A vote that matters needs a key from a ceremony. See
[The trusted setup](/docs/trust/trusted-setup).
</Callout>

The finished example is
[05-anonymous-voting](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/05-anonymous-voting)
in xark-examples. It adds a demo proof
([`circuits/vote/examples/demo.rs`](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/05-anonymous-voting/circuits/vote/examples/demo.rs))
bundled as the verifier crate's self-test, which this tutorial skips, a `close_poll` instruction
that lets the authority freeze the tally, host tests that feed each account check the accounts it
exists to refuse, and more refusals in its end-to-end test: a proof made against four options, a
vote in a closed poll.

Every vote here is the same size, one. The next tutorial goes back to 04's notes and lets them
carry any amount, which is where range proofs become essential: once amounts are private and
arbitrary, only a range check on each one stops a note from being worth "−1".
