xark's recursion story is built on the **BN254↔Grumpkin 2-cycle**. Grumpkin is
`y² = x³ − 17` over BN254's scalar field `Fr`, so Grumpkin coordinates are ordinary circuit
`Field`s and every Grumpkin group operation is a handful of **native** constraints. That makes the
compute-heavy half of folding verification cheap: a full Nova fold step verifies in-circuit in
**~9k constraints**, where non-native EC would cost millions.

Everything on this page is verified in the repository's test suite (accept + tamper-reject + a
Groth16 proof), and is exposed as runnable examples rather than a finished library API. There is
no closed IVC yet; the last section says exactly what is missing.

## What is built

All of these are `#[circuit]` examples under
[`examples/`](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark/tree/master/examples).

| Example | Verifies | Constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `grumpkin_ipa` | a deferred-MSM accumulator claim `Q = Σⱼ sⱼ·Gⱼ` | 12,212 |
| `grumpkin_ipa_fold` | the full Halo IPA reduction, with in-circuit Poseidon2 Fiat–Shamir | 40,150 |
| `grumpkin_nova_fold` | one Nova folding step | 8,859 |
| `grumpkin_ivc` | a 2-step folding IVC (computation + accumulator chains) | 18,200 |
| `grumpkin_complete_add` | identity-safe complete Grumpkin addition | 24 |

Alongside them, `grumpkin_nova_prover` is the **host-side prover core**: it extracts a Xark R1CS
`(A, B, C)`, folds committed relaxed instances (with the cross-term `T`), runs a multi-step
stepping loop, and uses a Grumpkin Pedersen commitment. The tests check that the folded relaxed
instance satisfies `Az ∘ Bz = u·Cz + E` and that the commitment fold is homomorphic.

## The cycle's field roles

Nova's primary circuit is over `Fr`, so a BN254 Groth16 proof can be its terminal proof. A witness
in field `K` can be committed homomorphically only with a curve whose **scalar field is `K`**:

- **BN254 G1** (scalar field `Fr`) commits the **primary (`Fr`) witness** — but its coordinates
  are in `Fq`, which is *non-native* in an `Fr` circuit.
- **Grumpkin** (scalar field `Fq`) commits the **companion (`Fq`) witness** — its coordinates are
  in `Fr`, which is *native* in an `Fr` circuit.

So the `Fr` circuit natively folds Grumpkin commitments (`grumpkin_nova_fold`) and **defers** the
one BN254-G1 scalar multiplication over the primary witness to a small companion circuit over `Fq`
— the **CycleFold** construction.

This role split is a measured finding, not theory: committing the primary `Fr` witness on Grumpkin
and folding it **fails**, because with `r·T` large, `r·T mod r ≠ r·T mod q` (Grumpkin's group
order is `Fq ≠ Fr`) and the `comm_E` fold mismatches. Grumpkin Pedersen is homomorphic over `Fq`,
confirming the split.

## The one capability gap

The CycleFold companion is a circuit over **`Fq`** — BN254's *base* field. **Xark is `Fr`-native**:
it compiles to a BN254-`Fr` R1CS for Groth16 and has no over-`Fq` target. So the companion circuit
is **not buildable in xark today**, and a *closed* IVC loop is not available.

What remains for a closed loop:

1. **CycleFold companion** — an `Fq`-field circuit / proof system. This is the capability gap;
   it is a new backend target, not an assembly step.
2. **Self-referential augmented step circuit `F′`** — computation + fold verification + Poseidon2
   I/O compression, whose own committed instance is folded next step. This is buildable on the
   `Fr` side; `grumpkin_augmented_step` is the shape.
3. **Decider** — a terminal SNARK opening the final folded commitment.

The verifier core and the host-side accumulation are done; #1 is genuinely new capability, while
#2–#3 are systems assembly with every field-matching pitfall already mapped.
