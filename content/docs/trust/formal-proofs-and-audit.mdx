<Callout type="error" title="Not audited. Experimental.">
xark has **not** been externally audited, and its on-chain verifier has **never been deployed to
mainnet**. Everything below is real and unusually rigorous for a backend at this stage — but formal
proofs and tests are *not* a substitute for an external audit. Treat xark as experimental. Don't hold
value on it.
</Callout>

With that said, the soundness story is a genuine differentiator, so here is exactly what it covers.

## Machine-checked with Lean 4

The gadgets and the ACIR→R1CS lowering carry **deductive proofs in Lean 4 against mathlib** — 29
modules, checked over *all* field assignments (no input bound). What's proven includes:

- **Field primitives:** booleanity, range decomposition (unique for `n ≤ 253` bits — the load-bearing
  `MAX_BITS`), bit-uniqueness.
- **Gadgets:** AND/XOR/NOT, Poseidon2 (S-box and the full BN254 permutation with its round constants
  transcribed), the Grumpkin point-addition gadget, scalar-mul ladders, non-native field arithmetic,
  and ECDSA verification against the textbook FIPS 186-4 relation, specialized to secp256k1/r1.
- **The lowering itself:** a meta-theorem that `AssertZero`, black-box dispatch, `Call` inlining, and
  memory scoping lower soundly, plus per-opcode end-to-end wrappers.
- **Brillig:** that ignoring the unconstrained hint opcodes is sound once the runtime pinning check
  passes (see [supported opcodes](/docs/reference/supported-opcodes#brillig-hints)).

The trust residue inside Lean is small and explicit: **three `axiom`s** (256-bit primality of the
secp256k1, secp256r1, and BN254 field orders, which Lean's kernel can't `decide`), plus mathlib's
standard axioms and `native_decide`. There is **no `sorry`** — CI rejects any proof depending on
`sorryAx`.

## Bounded-checked with Kani

The on-chain verifier's byte logic — canonicality, fail-closed behavior, strict non-malleability,
arity, and pairing-operand assembly — is checked with **Kani bounded model checking**. That's a
different, complementary guarantee from the unbounded Lean proofs: exhaustive within input bounds
rather than deductive over all inputs.

## Tested against reality

- **Differential tests against snarkjs** — an independent Groth16 implementation verifies every
  proof xark produces ([snarkjs interop](/docs/reference/snarkjs)).
- **Known-answer vectors** — NIST/RFC test vectors for the hashes and curves.
- Roughly **290 tests** in all (integration + unit), including the ceremony end-to-end and the
  Brillig-pinning checks.

## What is trusted, not proven

Honesty about the edges matters as much as the proofs:

- **`nargo` / ACVM** — xark lowers ACIR faithfully, but a Noir compiler bug that under-constrains a
  Brillig hint is out of scope; the defence is the pinned Noir version.
- **Arkworks Groth16** — used as a dependency; there is no in-repo proof of the prover itself.
- **The Lean kernel + mathlib**, and the standard Groth16 trusted-setup assumption.
- **BN254 gives ~100 bits of security** against the best known attacks at this size. If you need
  more, don't use BN254 Groth16.
