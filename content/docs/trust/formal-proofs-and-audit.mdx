<Callout type="error" title="Not audited. Experimental.">
xark has **not** been externally audited, and its on-chain verifier has **never** been deployed to
mainnet. Everything below is real — but formal proofs and tests are *not* a substitute for an
external audit. Treat xark as experimental. Don't hold value on it.
</Callout>

With that said, here is exactly what the soundness story covers. The repository's
[`docs/audit-status.md`](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark/blob/master/docs/audit-status.md)
and [`docs/security.md`](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark/blob/master/docs/security.md) are the
full accounts.

## Machine-checked with Lean 4

The proofs live in `formal/`, in Lean 4 against mathlib, and CI checks them on every pull request.

- **Gadget templates, over the rows the compiler emits.** A gadget marked `#[gadget]` has its R1CS
  exported and compared with a Lean template, and the template carries a soundness theorem; CI
  fails if the gadget's rows drift from it, and checks which production calls emit exactly those
  rows. This covers bit decompositions, `xor` / `or`, `add32`, `reduce::<N>`, the hash bit
  functions (`xor3`, `maj3`, `chi`, `ch`), the AES S-box, Merkle's `cond_swap`, the Grumpkin scalar
  multiplications and Pedersen hash, and the signature gadgets' building blocks below.
- **Non-native arithmetic for the signature gadgets.** The pseudo-Mersenne multiplication
  certificates for secp256k1, P-256 and Ed25519, limb range checks and `require_lt`, the
  secp256k1 / P-256 point add, fused double-and-add and on-curve checks, Ed25519's curve checks, and
  the scalar side of fake GLV — all over their exported rows.
- **Field primitives and bitwise operations**, restated by hand: booleanity, range decomposition,
  bit uniqueness, AND / XOR / NOT.
- **Poseidon2:** the `x⁵` S-box rows, and determinism of the full BN254 `t = 3` permutation with
  `xark-poseidon2`'s constants and matrices.
- **The compiler's rewrites and minimizer.** Each constraint-reducing rewrite (elimination, CSE,
  inlining, folding, the guards on witness branches) is proven exact over an R1CS model, and every
  minimizer run is checked by a certificate checker proven sound and complete.

The trust base inside Lean is only the standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound`). There are **no** `axiom` declarations — the secp256k1, P-256 and BN254 primes are
proven prime with kernel-checked Pratt certificates — and no `sorry` and no `native_decide`: CI
rejects any proof that depends on anything else.

What isn't proven is said as plainly. The signature gadgets are proven step by step, but how the
steps are chained across the scalar-multiplication loop is covered by tests, not a whole-loop
proof. Some lemmas describe models no current gadget emits, and a few gadgets are pinned only by
a gate count matched to a hand-restated model. The audit-status document lists each.

## Bounded-checked with Kani

The on-chain verifier's byte logic — canonicality, fail-closed behavior, strict non-malleability,
arity, totality, and pairing-operand assembly — is checked with **Kani bounded model checking**.
That's a different, complementary guarantee from the Lean proofs: exhaustive within input bounds
rather than deductive over all inputs.

## Tested against reality

- **Known-answer cross-checks** — every gadget against its reference crate (`k256`, `p256`,
  `ed25519-dalek`, `sha2`, `keccak`, `blake2`, `blake3`, `aes`, `ark-grumpkin`), with tampering
  tests (mutate the witness, confirm the constraints reject it) and adversarial inputs (all-zero,
  all-FF, near-modulus, boundary-length).
- **Official vectors** — NIST/RFC test vectors for the hashes and AES (FIPS 180-4, FIPS 202,
  FIPS 197 / CAVP, RFC 7693, the official BLAKE3 vectors).
- **Exhaustive witness search** — a fuzzer compares the circuit's semantics with native Rust on
  generated programs, and every example's own test inputs (and mutations of them) are searched for
  any second witness. That covers whole circuits up to the full EdDSA verifier: every valid
  signature has exactly one witness, every mutated input none.
- **Groth16 cross-checks** — xark's Groth16 against `ark-groth16` as an independent
  implementation: byte-identical keys and proofs for the same randomness, and the same verdicts.
- **The syscalls** — the verifier's curve operations through the Arkworks fallback and through the
  real `alt_bn128` syscalls (in Mollusk) give byte-identical results.
- **Ceremony end-to-end** — every rejection path (Schnorr proof of contribution, transcript chain,
  δ-consistency), and a full CLI run that proves and verifies with a ceremony's keys.
- **Fuzzing** of the verifier's entry points and the `circuit.xbc` decoder: never panic, never
  accept.

## What is trusted, not proven

Honesty about the edges matters as much as the proofs:

- **`rustc`'s wasm32 codegen.** Every circuit operation is an opaque wasm import, so the circuit
  is the same under every `rustc` (CI compares every example's digest on 1.89, stable and beta), and
  the lowering is fuzzed against native Rust with exhaustive witness search. But the compiler from
  Rust to R1CS is not verified end to end.
- **xark's Groth16** (setup, prover, verifier) is in-repo and tested against `ark-groth16`, not
  mechanised.
- **BN254 arithmetic** (`ark-bn254`, `ark-ff`, `ark-ec`), the `alt_bn128` syscalls, the Lean kernel
  and mathlib, and the toolchains (`rustc`, `cargo`, `lake`, Kani).
- **The trusted-setup assumption** — see [The trusted setup](/docs/trust/trusted-setup).
- **Building a circuit runs its code.** `xark build` and `xark test` compile the crate, running its
  `build.rs` and proc-macros like `cargo build`. Don't build an untrusted circuit crate.
- **BN254 gives ~100 bits of security** against the best known attacks. If you need more, don't use
  BN254 Groth16.
