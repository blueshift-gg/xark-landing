<Callout type="error" title="Not audited. Experimental.">
xark has **not** been externally audited, and its on-chain verifier has **never** been deployed to
mainnet. Everything below is real and unusually rigorous for a backend at this stage — but formal
proofs and tests are *not* a substitute for an external audit. Treat xark as experimental. Don't hold
value on it.
</Callout>

With that said, the soundness story is a genuine differentiator, so here is exactly what it covers.

## Machine-checked with Lean 4

The R1CS gadgets xark lowers from its Rust gadget crates carry **deductive proofs in Lean 4 against
mathlib**, checked over *all* field assignments (no input bound). What's proven includes:

- **Field primitives:** booleanity, range decomposition (uniquely determined for widths `n ≤ 253`),
  bit-uniqueness.
- **Bitwise / carry gadgets:** AND/XOR/NOT, the XOR-parity and wrapping-add carry chains — the
  primitives SHA-256, Keccak, BLAKE2, BLAKE3, and AES compose out of.
- **Poseidon2:** the S-box and the full BN254 permutation (the `t = 3` instance the gadget
  implements, with its round constants and matrices transcribed), end-to-end determined.
- **Embedded curves:** Grumpkin point addition (full algebraic closure: generic + doubling + inverse
  + on-curve + selector mux), and secp256k1 / secp256r1 incomplete point-add.
- **Scalar multiplication:** the LSB-first double-and-add ladder, proven abstractly over any
  additive commutative group — including GLV decomposition and the comb / Strauss–Shamir tables.
- **Non-native arithmetic:** the prover-aided modular-multiply chain the ECDSA gadgets build on,
  with the full ℕ carry recurrence and the `Fr`-level no-wrap argument (`< 2^253 < r`).
- **ECDSA verification:** an end-to-end wrapper composing the per-primitive theorems against the
  textbook FIPS 186-4 relation, specialised to secp256k1 / secp256r1.
- **AES S-box:** the GF(2⁸) multiplicative-inverse trick + affine transform equals the FIPS-197
  lookup table for every byte.
- **Merkle membership:** the per-level sibling mux is a genuine conditional swap (no
  under-constraint).
- **The lowering & bookkeeping:** the xark-IR → R1CS meta-theorem, allocation idempotence /
  injectivity, and that public inputs are pinned — so the constraints the gadgets are proved against
  are the constraints the compiler actually emits.

The trust residue inside Lean is small and explicit: **three `axiom`s** (256-bit primality of the
secp256k1, secp256r1, and BN254 scalar moduli, which Lean's kernel can't `decide`), plus mathlib's
standard axioms and `native_decide` (used for the 256-byte AES GF(2⁸) identities). There is **no
`sorry`** — CI prints `#print axioms` for every load-bearing theorem and rejects any proof depending
on `sorryAx`.

## Bounded-checked with Kani

The on-chain verifier's byte logic — canonicality, fail-closed behavior, strict non-malleability,
arity, totality, and pairing-operand assembly — is checked with **Kani bounded model checking**.
That's a different, complementary guarantee from the unbounded Lean proofs: exhaustive within input
bounds rather than deductive over all inputs.

## Tested against reality

- **Differential tests against snarkjs** — an independent Groth16 implementation verifies every
  proof xark produces ([snarkjs interop](/docs/reference/snarkjs)).
- **KAT cross-checks** — every gadget is tested against its reference crate (`k256`, `p256`, `sha2`,
  `keccak`, `blake2`, `blake3`, `aes`, `ark-grumpkin`), including tampering tests (mutate the
  witness, confirm the constraint system reports unsatisfied) and adversarial inputs (all-zero,
  all-FF, near-modulus, boundary-length).
- **Official vectors** — NIST/RFC test vectors for the hashes and curves (FIPS 180-4, FIPS 202,
  FIPS 197 / CAVP, RFC 7693, the official BLAKE3 vectors).
- **The Lean ↔ R1CS bridge** — snapshot tests pin each gadget's multiplication-gate count to its
  Lean soundness model, so any drift forces a re-check.
- **Ceremony end-to-end** — every `CeremonyError` path (Schnorr PoK, transcript chain,
  δ-consistency, dev-mode guard) is exercised.
- **A `cargo-fuzz` smoke** over the artifact parser, witness parser, and IR→R1CS lowering (run
  manually; production fuzzing is CPU-weeks, not yet a CI gate).

## What is trusted, not proven

Honesty about the edges matters as much as the proofs:

- **`rustc` and the MIR extractor** — xark lowers the accepted MIR subset faithfully, but a bug in
  MIR extraction (or a construct slipping past subset validation) is out of scope for the Lean
  proofs. The defence is the pinned nightly, the strict subset validation that rejects everything
  outside the accepted MIR, and the fuzz harness over the lowering.
- **Arkworks Groth16** — used as a dependency; there is no in-repo proof of the prover itself.
- **The Lean kernel + mathlib**, and the standard Groth16 trusted-setup assumption.
- **BN254 gives ~100 bits of security** against the best known attacks at this size. If you need
  more, don't use BN254 Groth16.
