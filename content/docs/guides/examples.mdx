The [xark-examples](https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples) repo is a difficulty ladder: four
circuits, each introducing exactly one new idea, and every one is built, proved, and verified
end-to-end inside an in-process Solana VM (no validator, no network). Read them in order.

<Cards>
  <Card title="01 · Over 9000" href="https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/01-over-9000" description="Prove a secret number beats a bound. The range-proof primitive, zero public inputs, a Pinocchio verifier." />
  <Card title="02 · Age verification" href="https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/02-age-verification" description="Prove 18+ without a birthday. Binds a range check to a published commitment." />
  <Card title="03 · Shielded pool" href="https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/03-shielded-pool" description="Unlinkable deposit → withdrawal (multi-denomination Tornado++). Merkle membership, nullifiers, two circuits, the chain never hashes." />
  <Card title="04 · Shielded transfer" href="https://github.com/blueshift-gg/xark-examples/tree/main/04-shielded-transfer" description="Private, arbitrary-amount payments (Zcash-style). Notes, a key hierarchy, JoinSplit, and native SPL." />
</Cards>

## The pattern they share

Every example is the same loop from the [Quickstart](/docs/quickstart): a Rust circuit, `xark`
turning it into a proof and a verifier crate, and a small Solana program that calls
`verify_instruction_data`. The two shielded examples add one trick worth its own study — **the chain
never hashes.** All Poseidon happens inside the circuit; it proves that a new Merkle root correctly
extends the old one, so the on-chain program only stores roots and verifies proofs. Circuit and chain
agree by construction instead of by carefully matching two Poseidon implementations.

<Callout type="warn">
The examples are reference implementations for learning — unaudited, not for production. The shielded
ones especially are educational; privacy tooling carries real legal weight depending on your
jurisdiction.
</Callout>
